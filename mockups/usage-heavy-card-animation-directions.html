Usage Tab Heavy Card Animation Directions

Eighteen focused variations using the same Usage tab content. Options 1-5 explore heavy lift-table motion, options 6-10 iterate on shutter and dovetail reveals, options 11-15 test present-frame lock travel, and options 16-18 try smaller cards rising up, expanding, and locking into place.

Option 01 / floor cylinder / lift plate

Hydraulic Lift Plate

Cards rise from a floor slot with only six pixels of travel. The bottom-up reveal does most of the work, then the plate seats with a short pressure stop.

Option 02 / flush deck / elevator plate

Flush Deck Elevation

The cards do not translate at all. They emerge from the surface like a deck elevator, using a straight bottom mask and a heavy brightness lock.

Option 03 / guideposts / stepped jack

Guidepost Jack Raise

A stepped jack motion gives the lift a mechanical cadence without letting it float. Travel stays under five pixels while the mask rises in hard increments.

Option 04 / scissor lift / lockout

Scissor Lift Lockout

The cards push upward like a compact lift and hard-lock at the top. It has a tiny vertical press, then a compressed metal stop.

Option 05 / foundry table / platen rise

Foundry Table Rise

A heavier table version of the lift concept. The cards rise into view from below and shed their floor shadow as they settle into the surface.

Option 06 / favorite 7 / steel shutter

Steel Shutter Clamp

A tighter version of option 7. The cards do not travel; a straight shutter opens across the face and the edge clamps into its final border state.

Option 07 / favorite 4 / dovetail gate

Dovetail Gate Shear

This keeps the dovetail idea but removes the old lateral throw. The card is already seated, then a machined gate shears open in place.

Option 08 / favorite 7 / twin shutters

Twin Shutter Pinch

Two shutters open from the center line, creating a heavy gate feel with no body travel. The lock moment is a short border pressure pulse.

Option 09 / favorite 4+7 / pressure seal

Pressure Seal Seat

The content is present from the first frame, then the metal surface brightens and compresses slightly as if pressure is applied from below.

Option 10 / favorite 7 / no-travel lock

Static Lock Plate

The most restrained version. No mask travel and no card movement, just a low brightness state snapping into a locked steel plate.

Option 11 / present frame / lateral slide lock

Cold Plate Slide Lock

Cards are visible from the first frame, then travel left-to-right into a cold steel stop. The motion has real throw, a small rebound, and only a restrained edge pulse.

Option 12 / present frame / tempered drop seat

Tempered Drop Seat

The cards begin fully visible above the seat, drop into position, overshoot by two pixels, and settle. The green temper is faint so the travel carries the lock-in.

Option 13 / present frame / deadbolt throw

Deadbolt Surface Throw

The cards start visible to the right, throw into place like a bolt, then rebound against the final edge. Lighting stays almost neutral; the force comes from travel.

Option 14 / present frame / diagonal rail catch

Bolt Rail Catch

A heavier diagonal catch: cards start visible down-left, drive into the rail, and clamp back to zero. It reads more physical without introducing a hidden reveal.

Option 15 / present frame / press-fit dock

Press-Fit Dock

Cards start visible below the seat, press upward into the frame, rebound, then lock. The color change is deliberately small, with a warm edge only at the impact.

Option 16 / scale rise / lift block

Lift Block Expand

Cards start visible, smaller, and slightly low. They rise into place, expand just past full size, then compress into a hard metal lock.

Option 17 / scale rise / telescoping plate

Telescoping Plate Seat

A more mechanical version: the cards begin squatter, telescope upward into full height, over-expand by a hair, then settle flat.

Option 18 / scale rise / riser clamp

Riser Clamp Lock

The most restrained of the three: a short upward rise, a modest scale expansion, and a final clamp with minimal warm edge pressure.
